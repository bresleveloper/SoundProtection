Sound Protection game plan

Sound Protection will hold loud Zoom and YouTube sound under the cap you set. Your sketch comes first, then the steps in order.

The sound path you want

Your sketch, redrawn. The numbers are your example, with the cap at 25 dB.

The game plan

Already done Safe · changes nothing on your PC Needs your OK · changes your PC

Each “needs your OK” step is asked on its own, when its turn comes. Nothing else counts as a yes.

  1. 1

    Install the tools to build the plug-in

    Already done

    You approved it. Installed and checked.

  2. 2
    You are here

    Save today’s sound settings

    Safe · changes nothing on your PC

    Copy the sound settings to two files, and play a quiet test tone to confirm where the volume slider acts.

  3. 3

    Build the plug-in and test it on recordings

    Safe · changes nothing on your PC

    It is tested inside a test program, not inside Windows’ sound. The one-step undo, which puts every setting back, is written and rehearsed.

  4. 4

    First real try: a monitor’s sound output, no test key

    Needs your OK · changes your PC

    We ask you which monitor first. With nothing plugged into its audio jack and no speakers, you hear nothing. Sound pauses while Windows’ audio restarts. Test key: a Windows setting allowing plug-ins Microsoft hasn’t signed.

    Changes: plug-in added to one monitor output. Undo: one-step undo.

  5. 5

    Only if step 4 fails: the test key

    Needs your OK · changes your PC

    Turn the test key on, restart the PC (asked separately), then try step 4 again. Side effect: while the key is on, copy-protected video (some streaming services) may refuse to play.

    Changes: one Windows setting, then a PC restart. Undo: delete it, restart.

  6. 6

    Your Headphones, at low volume

    Needs your OK · changes your PC

    Headphones off your head, slider at 10% or less, a quiet test tone first. Then real Zoom calls and YouTube.

    Changes: plug-in added to the Headphones’ effects. Undo: one-step undo.

  7. 7

    The tray app becomes the remote control

    Needs your OK · changes your PC

    On and off, your cap, and alerts that say how far over the sound went.

    Changes: tray app starts with Windows. Undo: untick it in the menu.

  8. 8

    Close the gaps

    Needs your OK · changes your PC

    Stop apps from taking sole control of the Headphones (“exclusive mode”), so nothing slips past the plug-in.

    Changes: one Headphones setting. Undo: tick the box again.

If it doesn’t work

  • Plan B: Voicemeeter, a sound-mixer app with its own limiter; it needs no test key.
  • One-step undo: double-click the Undo icon on your desktop, say yes once, and every setting goes back.
