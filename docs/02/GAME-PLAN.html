Two ways to turn loud apps down

Recommended · the real cap

Way 1: our plug-in inside the sound engine

Turns each app down before it is mixed, and holds sudden bangs on the total. This is the drawing above.

Good

  • Every app, before the slider, as in your sketch.
  • Holds sudden bangs at once.
  • The slider stays free; the headphones stay at max.

Catches

  • Windows normally loads only plug-ins it has signed. Expect to need the “test key”, a Windows setting that allows unsigned ones. While it is on, some copy-protected video may refuse to play.
  • Windows or Realtek updates can knock it out. The tray checks after every start and offers a Repair.
  • “Audio enhancements: Off” very likely switches it off. The tray then warns you in red.
Quick start · no install

Way 2: the tray turns each loud app’s own volume down

Like a hand on each app’s slider in Windows’ Volume Mixer. It lowers a loud app, then lets it back up when it gets quiet.

Good

  • Nothing to install, no admin, no test key.
  • Per app, and the master slider stays free.
  • A similar new tool reports turning loud parts down in 10–30 ms.

Catches

  • The start of each loud part gets through, so it can’t stop a bang.
  • You’ll see the per-app sliders move.
  • If the tray app crashes, an app can stay low until the tray starts again and puts it back.

My suggestion: try Way 2 first, to feel the cap. Meanwhile I build Way 1 safely, on recordings. Then we take Way 1’s PC steps one OK at a time.

What “too loud” means

  • Loudness, not peaks. The leveler listens the way broadcasters measure loudness, over the last 0.4 seconds, and turns an app down smoothly. Quiet apps are never turned up.
  • Bangs. The safety cap sits a little above your cap and catches sudden peaks at once.
  • The number. Before the slider, the PC only knows a digital level: 0 is the loudest, and everything else is negative. I read your 25, 28, 35 and 17 as an example. I suggest the panel shows each app’s level and the cap on that one scale (for example, a cap of −20), so you set it by watching a loud call.

What we find out on the way

  • Does an app’s meter read before or after its own volume?Step 3Unknown. The quick version handles both.
  • Does Windows load our plug-in without the test key?Step 6I expect not.
  • Does anything you watch stop playing with the key on?Step 7Zoom and YouTube should be fine. Some paid streaming may refuse.
  • Do Zoom and YouTube both pass the per-app part?Step 8YouTube should. Zoom is unknown; if it skips it, the safety cap still holds Zoom.
  • Does “Audio enhancements: Off” switch our plug-in off?Step 10Probably yes, so the tray warns you.
  • Do Windows or Realtek updates remove it?Every startSometimes, so the tray offers a Repair.

If it doesn’t work

  • Way 2 alone: the quick version needs no test key.
  • Plan B: Voicemeeter, a sound-mixer app with its own limiter. It needs no test key, but your Windows slider would then come before the cap.
  • One-step undo: double-click the Undo icon on your desktop, say yes once, and every setting goes back.
